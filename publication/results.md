# Results

## 1. Canonical CALR protein reconstruction

The computational protein layer uses CALR protein reference NP_004334.1 (417 amino acids) and reconstructs the canonical MPN-associated exon 9 frameshift consequences. Type 1 (`p.L367fs*46`) produces a 411-amino-acid mutant sequence, while Type 2 (`p.K385fs*47`) produces a 430-amino-acid mutant sequence.

The direction of these length changes is consistent with the molecular definitions of the 52-bp deletion and 5-bp insertion. The reconstructed sequences therefore provide a transparent computational representation of the two canonical classes rather than relying only on textual HGVS labels.

## 2. C-terminal remodeling

The wild-type C terminus is strongly enriched for acidic residues. In the repository's residue-count proxy, wild-type CALR contains 50 K/R residues and 109 D/E residues, giving a net charge proxy of −59. Type 1 contains 59 K/R and 77 D/E residues (−18), whereas Type 2 contains 64 K/R and 88 D/E residues (−24).

These counts are not formal physicochemical measurements; they are an auditable residue-composition summary. They demonstrate the direction of C-terminal remodeling expected after the exon 9 frameshift: substantial reduction of acidic character and increased relative basic character.

## 3. Shared and distinct mutant consequences

Type 1 and Type 2 both replace the normal C-terminal sequence with a novel mutant tail. Their upstream mutant regions are not identical, however, because the underlying indels occur at different positions and remove different amounts of the wild-type sequence. This distinction is biologically relevant and is one reason the project does not collapse all CALR mutations into a single category.

Published work supports the classification of additional exon 9 indels as type 1-like or type 2-like according to the extent and pattern of C-terminal remodeling. Recent diagnostic studies further show that non-standard indels may be mischaracterized when only fragment size is considered, supporting sequence-level clarification where appropriate. citeturn0search2turn0search10

## 4. Diagnostic-method implications

The computational evidence supports a research workflow in which PCR/capillary electrophoresis can serve as a screening layer, while unusual exon 9 profiles are resolved by sequence-based methods. This is consistent with recent concordance data showing discrepancies between fragment sizing and NGS for some non-standard CALR indels. citeturn0search10

The project therefore treats Type 1 and Type 2 as canonical benchmarks and explicitly reserves a separate pathway for non-standard variants.

## 5. Biological interpretation

CALR exon 9 mutations are strongly associated with BCR::ABL1-negative ET and PMF and are particularly relevant in cases without JAK2 or MPL driver mutations. The canonical mutations create a +1 frameshift and an altered C-terminal region lacking the normal KDEL retention signal; current reviews describe altered MPL/JAK-STAT signaling as a central pathogenic consequence. citeturn0search0turn0search5

These findings are computational reconstructions and evidence synthesis, not patient-level observations or clinical assay validation.
