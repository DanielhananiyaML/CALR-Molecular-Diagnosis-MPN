# Sequence-Derived Results

## Protein reconstruction

The CALR protein reference used in this analysis is NP_004334.1 (417 amino acids). The repository reconstructs the two canonical exon 9 MPN-associated protein consequences against this reference: Type 1, p.L367fs*46, producing a 411-aa mutant protein; and Type 2, p.K385fs*47, producing a 430-aa mutant protein.

Both canonical classes are frameshift consequences of exon 9 indels. A large collaborative ET study reported that Type 1 (52-bp deletion) and Type 2 (5-bp TTGTC insertion) together represented more than 80% of CALR mutations in that cohort. The current analysis uses those canonical classes as molecular benchmarks rather than treating them as the complete spectrum of CALR variation.

## C-terminal remodeling

The reconstructed mutants replace the normal acidic C-terminal region with novel, arginine/lysine-rich sequences. The charge analysis in `calr_protein_charge_metrics.tsv` is a transparent residue-count proxy; it is not a formal prediction of isoelectric point or a clinical functional assay.

## Diagnostic interpretation

The computational result supports the molecular sequence: **exon 9 indel → reading-frame disruption → altered C-terminal sequence**. Published studies demonstrate that CALR Type 1 and Type 2 mutations have distinct biological and clinical associations, while recent diagnostic work shows that non-Type I/II indels can create interpretation problems and may require sequence-level clarification.

These results are computational reconstructions and evidence synthesis. They are not patient-level findings and do not constitute clinical assay validation or a diagnostic report.
