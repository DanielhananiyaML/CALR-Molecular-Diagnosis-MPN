# Discussion

The CALR project demonstrates how a short exon 9 indel can be translated computationally into a protein-level molecular phenotype. Rather than treating the mutation as a database label alone, the workflow connects HGVS representation, reading-frame change, protein reconstruction and C-terminal composition.

The reconstructed Type 1 and Type 2 proteins show the expected qualitative remodeling of the CALR C terminus. This matters because the C-terminal region contains features central to CALR biology, including acidic calcium-binding character and the endoplasmic-reticulum retention signal. Reviews of CALR-mutated MPNs describe the mutant tail as a positively charged neomorphic sequence lacking KDEL and link mutant CALR to aberrant MPL/JAK2/STAT signaling. citeturn0search0turn0search5

The project also highlights an important diagnostic limitation: the CALR mutation spectrum is broader than the two canonical variants. More than 50 exon 9 lesions have been described, and the literature commonly groups non-canonical lesions as type 1-like or type 2-like according to their molecular resemblance. citeturn0search1turn0search3

This distinction has practical laboratory implications. A 2026 concordance study found that some non-standard CALR indels produced discordant fragment-size interpretations when compared with NGS, supporting reflex sequencing for unusual profiles. citeturn0search10 A separate 2026 diagnostic series also reported that a subset of non-Type I/II CALR variants were germline rather than somatic, emphasizing that rare variants should not automatically be interpreted as MPN driver mutations. citeturn0search13

The project's main strength is therefore not simply that it reproduces two well-known mutations. It creates an extensible framework in which common variants, rare exon 9 indels, reference-version differences and diagnostic evidence can be represented separately. This is particularly appropriate for a bioinformatics-oriented molecular diagnostics portfolio.

## Limitations

The current protein layer should be interpreted as a computational reconstruction/benchmark rather than a laboratory validation study. The charge analysis is a residue-count proxy and does not estimate experimental pI, folding energetics, receptor binding, or cellular phenotype. The project also does not yet contain patient-level CALR sequencing data or a prospective diagnostic-performance study.

A future public-data analysis can extend the work by examining CALR-mutated ET/PMF cohorts with clearly documented provenance, variant definitions and clinical metadata.
